Known unfinished work

This guide separates two different jobs: transcription means typing what a source page says; translation means rendering Welsh text in English. Names and place names should be preserved as written, with normalized forms recorded separately as finding aids.

These statements come from handwritten and typed project notes dated 2007. “Not yet online” does not mean a source is lost. The full PC and flash-drive edition can use the links below to search the local archive; the GitHub starter records the work even when the images cannot yet be opened publicly.

Branch-name discovery

Possible additions to the comparison registry

Unfinished minutes can preserve branch names that are absent from the original membership-CD index. A volunteer does not need to transcribe an entire volume before reporting a name: record the spelling exactly as seen, page or image number, date, and nearby conference or place context.

The 2007 meeting-transcript checklist has already supplied additional local evidence for Pen-y-Darran, Stepaside, and Pontypool. It also confirms Gymmer as a spelling associated with Cymmer. These have been added to the comparison registry as candidates requiring human and FamilySearch review.

Branch-specific transcripts

Long-term goal: finish and review the partial transcript and translation collection, then reorganize it as clean researcher-facing source material. Exclude internal work-in-progress or front-matter pages from the final presentation where appropriate while preserving the original private working files.

Map minutes, histories, blessings, conference material, and other narrative records to the applicable canonical branches. Where material exists, add a branch-specific Transcripts section linking only the relevant material and preserving its provenance and original page or image relationships. Do not imply that every branch has transcript material, and do not implement these links broadly until the collection review is complete.
